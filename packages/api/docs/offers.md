# Offers and trades

Part of `packages/api/AGENTS.md`, which points here.

## Offer get sources

### Where a get item comes from

- A get item comes from the partner's lists while they have any (`partnerHasLists`), and `createOffer` checks nothing else bar the recipient's offer rules (below). Those lists are `askableOnTrade` (`services/trade/lists.ts`): have or sale, bound, and put on Trade, idle or not; a bound list kept off Trade is never asked from.
- Where an offer starts never narrows the get side, except that every ask comes from one post for a partner with lists (below).
- `fromList` narrows the theirs picker through `allowedEntries` (and skips kept counter items, which sit on no list); the send check is unchanged.
- The whole theirs list (`offer.candidates` without an offset) adds the partner's lists on Trade as `lists` (`partnerListChoices`) for the web's List choice; `suggestOffer` skips that query.
- An any-copy ask fits every entry of its collection (`entriesFitting`), also one that names a copy on a list that shows serials, and draws on the copies the entries name, or on every copy with a whole-collection entry (`anyCopyScope`). The any item's `hidden` says how many of its `copies` only any serial reaches, those no shown, sendable serial in scope stands for; `itemsOnLists` matches an any item only to an entry that lists the collection as any copy (`entryObjektId` null), so a match through a list that only names copies adds one of them; in `onePostVerdict` an any ask that names its list is a candidate of that list alone (`askCandidates`), so a serial on one post beside any copies picked from another stays an offer across posts, so an any ask draws only on the chosen post's copies and `checkItems` refuses asks that need copies from two lists, and `listedAsAny` (`schemas/offer.ts`) decides whether Make offer for one objekt keeps the any ask. A picker page never splits a collection: `theirPickerPage` cuts through `pageByCollection` and the owned reads through `ownedGroupPage`, a collection over `CANDIDATE_PAGE_SIZE` being a page of its own. `OFFER_SIDE_LIMIT` stays at or below `CANDIDATE_PAGE_SIZE`: a counter's give picks take their flags from one unpaged `offer.candidates` read (`objektIds`, through `ownedPage`), which returns at most one page, so raising the limit past the page size needs that read paged first. A counter's any-copy asks take their copies from one `copiesOf` read of every copy of their collections, never one read per ask, since `offer.candidates` allows 120 calls a minute per user.
- With none, and only while the partner has `allow_collection_asks` on (off by default), the get side may draw on what the partner holds at any linked address (`collectionAddresses`), by specific objekt only, since every copy there shows its serial: the same set the picker offers. `refusedCollectionAsks` (`services/offer/collection.ts`) checks this at send, with `collectionAskAllowed` as the pure rule; the `theirs-collection` candidates side (`theirCollectionPage`) refuses `not_listed` for a partner who has lists, so a stale tab can't bypass them. A collection item keeps `list_id` null, as a kept counter item does.

### The recipient's offer rules

- The recipient's offer rules are two `user` switches, `only_wanted_offers` and `allow_collection_asks`, off by default, plus one post per offer, which is always on and has no switch (`user.multi_post_asks` is retired, no longer read or written). `createOffer` reads the switches through `offerRulesOf` and applies all three before `checkMessageRate`, which spends a send slot, through the pure `lib/offer-recipient-rules.ts` (tested).
- Only offers with what I want refuses `not_wanted` for a give off one want list, chosen by the pure `givesRuleOf` (table in `web-trade-offers` Recipients who take only what they want): a new ask or a gives-only offer on a post is held to that post's want list (`postWantedAmong`); a post without a want list, or an empty one, takes no objekts; a `wantList` start (resolved by `resolveWantList`, the partner's discoverable want list with entries) is held to it (`wantListWantedAmong`); every discoverable want list (`wantedAmong`) only for a partner with no lists, a counter asking nothing new, and a request naming no post. `givesRuleOf` also returns what `post_list_id` stores.
- The builder reads each post's want list from `PartnerList.want` and keeps the picker to it with `wantList`. The offer view's "Not on your wants" label reads every want list instead, since only the recipient sees it.
- One post per offer, for a partner with a have or sale list on Trade, refuses `other_post` unless every ask comes through one `askableOnTrade` list (`onePostVerdict`); it never trusts the client's `post` alone, which a sender can leave out by offering from chat.
- A closed collection refuses `not_listed`, the code tabs from before it already know.
- A counter's kept objekts are exempt from all three, keyed by objekt id, since a kept objekt that is also listed gets a `list_id` from `matchEntry`; a give the recipient asked for in the countered offer is exempt from the wanted rule (`counteredAsks`).
- `offer.post_list_id` records the post: the client's `post` (resolved by `resolvePostList`, null rather than a refusal) while every ask fits it, else the one list the asks came through, for "Offer on {post}"; `givesRuleOf`'s `stored`, which is null for a counter asking nothing new and for a gives-only offer with no `post` and no `wantList`. A gives-only offer naming a Buy post (`wantList`) stores that want list, and `post_id` the row it leads. `offer.post_id` stores, beside it, the lowest `trade_post` row that list leads, resolved inside the insert, and is NULL with it or when the list leads no row. `post_list_id` is the offer's history and every reader stays on it; `post_id` is an address for later per-post features, and nothing reads it yet. The two part ways in four cases, so `post_id` is never read as history: the lead list is deleted while its want list is on Trade (the row is re-led to the want list, `post_list_id` goes NULL and the "Offer on" line drops); a have list that was off Trade is merged into a live Buy post (its old row is deleted, `post_id` goes NULL, `post_list_id` keeps the list); and the same merge when that have list's old want list is on Trade, which takes over the old row, so `post_id` stays set on a post led by another list. Fourth, a Buy post later paired with a have list is re-led by that list, so an offer stored on the Buy post keeps `post_list_id` on the want half while `post_id` stays on the row; `trade-post-drift`'s first note counts these.
- The builder's whole theirs page (`offer.candidates`, side `theirs`, no offset) carries `partnerRules` (`partnerRulesOf`), which the builder follows, and `wantLists` (the partner's discoverable want lists with entries, from the same statement as `lists`, which a start may name, each with `buy`, true for a Buy post, computed with `resolveWantList`'s predicate: no `trade_post` row pairs with it); scroll pages don't, and a closed collection is checked by `allow_collection_asks` alone.
- `fetchOffers(ids, reader)` runs the label and nudge checks only for offers sent to `reader`; it runs no post checks, and an offer open across posts from before the rule shows no mark.
- `user.setOfferRules` serves the wanted-rule decline nudge and Account › Trade, one switch at a time, and returns without a write when nothing is set (a tab from before sends `multiPostAsks`, which the schema strips); `user.updateAccount` needs the whole General form and sends no offer rule.

### Suggested offers

- `suggestOffer` (`offer.suggest`) reads the person-row pipeline: `recountPartners` for the one partner under the row's Compare with sides, then fills by the rule the row's chip follows (`fillMatches`): with a Can swap person, opened without a post or from the person's post in the counted pair, the `swapPair` matches both ways, each cut to the pair's lists; otherwise only the side with more matches through the post (or all), You get on a tie, swaps and sales alike (no top-up is set for a sale).
- For every partner with lists, a row's You get matches outside a pair keep only the partner list with the most (`rowPost`, a tie going to the row's `lead`, then list order), returned as `postSlug`; the side with more matches is chosen after that cut.
- You give is cut the same way to one want list (`oneListOf` over the partner's discoverable want lists, read with their askable lists in `suggestOffer`'s first batch, so the cut adds no round trip). Both cuts start from the uncut sides, since `fillMatches` has already emptied the smaller one; when You give wins, the result carries that list's name as `postName`, no `postSlug`, and its slug as `wantSlug`.
- Near matches still skip every match through the post, the unfilled side's too.
- Each get item is matched back to its match's list entry by `matchEntry`, the rule sending uses (`itemsOnLists`), since the picker keeps one list per objekt and two lists can hold the same copy.
- It returns the picks `chooseMatchPicks` (`lib/offer-prefill.ts`) chooses and `matched` per side, the count before the side limit; with `post`, only that post's lists (`postSides`) and the post's name. The narrowing is pure, in `lib/offer-prefill.ts`, beside its tests.

## Estimated serials

`serialEstimated` (and a `ShownSerial`'s `estimated`) comes from `isSerialEstimated(mintedAt, anchored)` (`@repo/lib/serial`), with `anchored` read through `anchoredSerialIds` (`services/objekt/serial-anchored.ts`), never from mint time alone: a post-cutoff objekt whose reserved range also holds a pre-cutoff serial or a manual anchor has an exact serial. Load the set once for the objekts a read returns, beside its other reads, as the pickers, `shownSerials` and `hydrateCards` do. The query mirrors how the worker pins a range (`apps/worker/docs/offline-serials.md`).

## Wrong copies

A wrong copy is accepted in one place: `acceptCopy` (`services/offer/verify.ts`), under the trade's row lock. The receiver's Accept (`acceptSubstitute`, `auto: false`) and the worker's deadline, expiry and break (`auto: true`, which sets `auto_accepted`) all call it, and `finishSettle` ends the trade either way. It lives in `offer/verify.ts` because the worker can import that module and not `services/offer/substitute.ts`, whose chain reaches `env.ts`.

## Legs marked as received

The receiver can close a waiting leg by hand (`confirmLeg`, `services/offer/confirm.ts`), under the trade's row lock and through `finishSettle`, as Accept on a wrong copy does. The leg gets `open = false`, `verified_at` and `confirmed_at`, and no `tx_hash`, `verified_objekt_id` or `transfer_id`, so every "done" count that reads `verified_at` (progress, the cancel lock, `tradeOutcome`, the unfinished rule) takes it unchanged. A verified leg therefore may have no hash. Only three reads tell a confirmed leg apart, through `confirmed_at`: reputation's verified count (`done` skips any trade with one), `rateRefusal` (`confirmedLegs`, refusing `confirmed_trade` on a completed trade only, so a failed one keeps the deliverer's rating; every caller passes it, `readDeals` included) and the trade read's `confirmedAt`. A pending wrong copy on the leg refuses `copy_pending`: the copy is decided first, since the worker's copy jobs skip a closed leg and would leave it pending. The giver gets `leg_confirmed` unless the confirmation ends the trade, which sends `completed` to both instead. `finishSettle` marks any `completed` note `confirmed: true` when a leg of the trade was confirmed, whoever closed the last leg, and the popover offers no Leave feedback on it.

## Received objekts

A leg is marked verified in three places, each under the trade's row lock, and each calls `removeReceived` (`services/list/received.ts`) for the legs it just verified, in the same transaction: the worker's verifier loop (a transfer), `acceptCopy` (a different copy accepted by the receiver or by the worker, at a deadline, a break or expiry) and `confirmLeg`. It deletes the leg's collection from the receiver's want lists whose `remove_on_receive` is on, hand-added entries only (a rule's, `rule_id`, are the rule's) and only those older than the leg's `verified_at`. It is given leg ids, never the trade, so a list switched on later or an entry added back is not reached by an older leg. It runs whether or not the trade then completes, writes no note, and leaves `updated_at` and the trade version alone, so cached For you matches catch up when they expire. It is not in `finishSettle`: an expiry that accepts a copy and still ends the trade unfinished never calls that. A new place that sets `verified_at` calls `removeReceived` too.

The trade page offers the same for lists with the switch off: `fetchTrade` puts `wantedOn` (`stillWantedOn`) on each verified leg the viewer received, naming their want lists that held the collection by hand before the leg verified, and `list.dropReceived` takes the collections it is given off one list.

## Cancel by agreement

Once Cancel trade locks, the parties end a trade together through a request (`services/offer/cancel-request.ts`): `offer.askCancel`, `answerCancel` and `withdrawCancel`. Every request is a `trade_cancel_request` row, kept for the Timeline and the 24-hour wait after a decline (`cancelAskWait`, keyed on the decline's `answered_at`); a partial unique index holds one `pending` row per trade. Each procedure locks the trade row `FOR UPDATE` before reading or writing a request, the order the verifier and `cancelTrade` take. Accept ends the trade through `endTradeCancelled` (`services/offer/trades.ts`), the one ending write `cancelTrade` shares: status `cancelled`, every leg closed, any pending wrong copy declined, with reason `agreed` and `cancelled_by` the asker. Nobody closes a pending row when the trade ends another way, so every read gates on the trade being `in_progress`: `fetchTrade` reports such a row as `lapsed` (the pure `cancelRequestViews`, `lib/offer-rules.ts`), and `readDeals` and `needsYouSql` skip it. Who may answer or withdraw is the pure `cancelAnswerRefusal` (`lib/offer-rules.ts`). Ask and withdraw share one per-user limit (`trade-cancel-ask`, 6 an hour), so a loop can't add a Timeline row and a partner notice each round. While a request is open it outranks a different copy and Your turn, so `needsYouSql`'s leg branch skips a trade with a pending request, as `dealOf` does.
